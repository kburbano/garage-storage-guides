Who this is for

You typed something like “garage cabinets with workbench,” “garage storage cabinets with built-in workbench,” or “best garage workbench with drawers.” You want enclosed storage and a top in the same buy, not a bare shelf and not a head-term tour of every cabinet Home Depot sells. The sets priced in full below land about $970–$2,270 before tax and delivery.

Hand tools you grab while you stand at the bench can live in drawers. Brooms, a hose, and light hooks still belong on the wall — see garage slatwall. Seasonal bins belong overhead, on racks for a low ceiling or, with a taller ceiling, a motorized storage lift. A ladder that already falls into the walkway needs its own hanger, not a cabinet shelf — see ladder storage.

The laundry room next to this project has its own cabinet height. Wall boxes over a washer and dryer are measured from the lid and the folding counter, not from a car door. That guide is cabinets over a washer and dryer.

Heat for the person standing at this bench is a different buy. A 2-car bay needs a heater sized in watts, aimed at the bench and clear of the door tracks. That guide is a garage heater for a 2-car garage.

Depth, height, and the car door

Two measurements decide the set. The first is the open car door against the wall where the cabinets will stand. The second is the height of the top relative to your elbow, not relative to a catalog photo. Do both before you care about the finish.

Depth against the parked car

Park the car where it actually lives. Open the door you use. Measure from the wall to the inside face of that door, then leave room to walk past the door and past your own feet. Bold’s published depth is 18 in. The Armadillo 7-piece footprint is also 18 in deep. The Pro set page says 24 in. The separate Pro 28 in tool cabinet page says that cabinet is 22 in deep, so the set’s 24 in is not the depth of every piece in the list. Seville’s 11-drawer bench is 20 in deep (77 in wide once the push bars are on) and the tall cabinets in that bundle are 18 in deep. The Seville 48 in bench is 24 in deep. Duramax’s bench is 24 in deep and its wall cabinets are 14 in deep.

The open wall above that run is a different buy. Brooms and the tools you grab while you stand there belong on Proslat or Gladiator slatwall, which does not stick 18–24 inches into the stall.

An r/garageporn owner who installed a Bold 9-piece with a bamboo top said the 18 in depth was the reason the car still fit in a small garage. That is one garage, not a clearance spec. A 24 in run on the same wall can be the difference between a door that opens and a door that stops on a cabinet.

Workbench height

On r/HomeImprovement (February 2023) people answering “what height and depth?” did not agree on one number, and they should not have. One woodworker who is 5 ft 10 in said they work at 35 in. Another suggested about 34 in high and 24–28 in deep against a wall, deep enough to work and shallow enough to reach tools hung above it. Another called belt-buckle height a rule of thumb: lower when you need leverage on a large piece, higher for small work. None of that is a manufacturer spec.

  • Seville 48 in bench (in the 4-piece). 28.5–42 in, in 1 in steps, four locking knobs. That range covers a seated setup and the standing heights in that thread. The bench page says to treat the load as 1,000 lb evenly distributed. Wheels are a separate 3 in set, not included.
  • Seville 11-drawer bench. Fixed at 37.5 in. You do not get the seated end of the range. You do get drawers and 5 in casters, two of them locking.
  • Duramax bench. The set page lists 37 in high. It does not say the height adjusts.
  • Armadillo 7-piece. The set page prints 90.8 in as the overall height, which is the locker plus the 18 in extender, and 35.1 in for the 3-drawer cabinet. It does not print the height of the hardwood top once it is installed. Armadillo’s own organization article says, “A typical garage workbench height lands between 34 and 38 inches.” That sentence is about a typical bench, not a measured height for this set. The same article’s 1,500 lb line is for the standalone 56 in and 84 in workbenches, not for this cabinet set.
  • NewAge Bold. The published height is the locker: 72 in, or 75.25–76.75 in with the legs in the set-dimension block. A FAQ answer on the same page says the legs bring the cabinets to 75.3–76.8 in. Those two ranges are not the same. The set page does not publish the work-surface height. Do not borrow a different NewAge workbench’s height range and apply it to 73509.
  • NewAge Pro tool cabinet. Its own page says 28 in W × 22 in D × 32.31 in H, and 35.6–37.06 in with the legs. The set description says the worktop is 1.25 in thick. Adding those published numbers puts a top on that cabinet near 36.8–38.3 in. That addition is not a published work-surface height. The locker FAQ on the same set page says a 36 in locker is 80 in, and 83.25–84.75 in with legs.

Outlets, slopes, and the wall

An outlet hidden behind an 18–24 in cabinet is not a convenient outlet. Bold’s workbench description mentions a cable-management opening. The set FAQ says the workbench is designed to be attached to the locker and the wall, and that you assemble the workbench and secure the full set to the wall. The hardware NewAge ships is for studs. A concrete wall needs different anchors, and the FAQ says to buy those locally. Seville’s rolling bench can be moved to the cord. NewAge’s Pro FAQ says casters on a base cabinet keep that cabinet from fitting under an installed worktop. Wheels and a fixed top are a choice, not a free extra.

Leveling on the Bold and Pro pages is 1.5 in: legs from 3.25 to 4.75 in. That fixes a local rock. It does not erase a slab that falls several inches from the back of the garage to the door. An r/garageporn owner planning a long Pro run in January 2024 described about 4 in of drop along the wall and was worried the feet covered less than that. The useful reply was to start at the high end and level each cabinet as you go, because the slope is spread across the run, not stacked under one box. NewAge’s support page says doors and drawers that will not close are a leveling problem first. The same page says Bold and Pro doors do not have adjustable hinges. Performance Plus is the line that does.

The slab under those feet is a separate buy. Interlocking tiles in the floor tiles vs epoxy guide are 3/8 in (Nitro) to 0.75 in (Swisstrax Ribtrax PRO). Garage Flooring LLC’s tile install sheet says to leave at least a 1/2 in gap at cabinets and to lay tiles around heavy equipment rather than under it, because a heavy box pins the tiles while the rest of the floor moves with temperature. Set the cabinets on the concrete and tile up to that gap, or accept the run as a clamp. A coating does not add that thickness, and it does not come back up. Level the cabinets to the floor you will actually have.

Owner pain points

These are paraphrases from threads and from NewAge’s own support page. They are not quotations, and they are not lab tests.

  • Thin steel and doors that bang. On The Garage Journal (January 2019) a shop owner looking at Gladiator and Kobalt tall cabinets on a store shelf thought the doors looked thin and not very rigid, and thought the NewAge cabinets they could see in photos were only 18 in deep against 20 in-plus on other brands. A reply said Bold was unexceptional and that Pro lockers are 24 in deep. Another owner with a lot of Gladiator cabinets agreed the doors are the flimsy part and that the frames and shelves are where the steel went. On r/garageporn (2024) an owner who had just installed a Bold 9-piece said the metal dented and scratched easily, the doors did not close smoothly and banged, the light color showed fingerprints, a hanger rod arrived bent and could be straightened by hand, and some washers showed rust. They were storing sports gear and garden tools, and they still preferred that to a custom wood quote. In a July 2025 r/garageporn thread, the original poster called their existing Craftsman cabinets flimsy. Replies split: one called Husky heavy-duty floor cabinets sturdy enough for home use and said the bad reviews they had read were about shipping; one was happy with Seville bought at Sam’s; two called NewAge Pro the opposite of flimsy, including one dent that was replaced after photos; one person who owns Seville thought the NewAge sample at Lowe’s felt thinner.
  • Door alignment is mostly the floor. NewAge’s support page says Bold and Pro do not have adjustable hinges, and that doors or drawers that will not close need the cabinet leveled and squared on the feet. There is no hinge screw that will fix a racked box. The 1.5 in of leg thread is the adjustment you get.
  • Assembly is the workbench, plus the wall. Bold’s FAQ says you assemble the workbench and you fasten the set to the wall. Seville includes the tools and still ships a 250 lb drawer bench to the curb. This check did not find a trustworthy hour count, so it does not invent one. Plan on two people for the heavy pieces. Seville’s older 72 in adjustable-bench instructions (model 20141, a different bench) already say two people. The current 48 in page says assembly is required and does not repeat that sentence.
  • Worktop material, not a documented sag. This pass did not find a thread of these NewAge, Seville, or Duramax tops sagging under load. What it did find, on r/garageporn (April 2023), was a split on stainless versus bamboo: one owner has both and said stainless scratches and dents the way a work surface will, and that they would pick stainless around cars and fluids and bamboo for wood. Another owner had two bamboo tops that did not match in color and got a replacement from customer service. A separate r/Workbenches post about a crowned top was a different brand, a Ramia bench in an unheated shed. Do not import that failure onto these sets. Do read the load line you actually have. Seville’s 1,000 lb is “evenly distributed.” A vise or a small engine in the middle of a top is not that test. NewAge’s support pages include a bench-vise install video, which is permission to mount a vise, not a new capacity number.
  • Wheels. Seville’s 11-drawer bench includes 5 in casters. NewAge tells you not to put casters on lockers, and tells you a base cabinet on casters will not fit under a worktop that is already installed. If “garage storage cabinets with wheels” is the query, buy the rolling bench, not a locker you have decided to mobilize.

Who should skip it

  • A one-car garage where the door already barely opens. If the open door is inside 20 in of the wall, Bold’s 18 in and the Armadillo 7-piece’s 18 in might work and the 24 in sets will not. If the door is inside 18 in, none of the floor cabinets here belong on that wall. The Armadillo footprint is also 90.8 in tall once the extender is on. Use the end wall, or keep the floor clear and hang storage: slatwall, overhead racks, and a ladder hanger do not stick 18–24 in into the stall.

FAQ

What height should a garage workbench be?

Armadillo’s organization article says, “A typical garage workbench height lands between 34 and 38 inches,” and tells you to choose a height near two inches below your elbow with shoes on. That is their general line, not a measured height for the 7-piece set. Owners on r/HomeImprovement described about 34–35 in for standing woodwork, and belt-buckle height as a rule of thumb that moves with the task. The only sets in this check that publish a range are Seville’s adjustable benches, 28.5–42 in. The 11-drawer Seville bench is 37.5 in. Duramax lists 37 in. Bold and the Armadillo set page do not publish a work-surface height at all.

How deep can garage cabinets with a workbench be and still let the car door open?

Measure the open door to the wall. Bold is 18 in. The Armadillo 7-piece footprint is 18 in. The Pro set is published at 24 in. Seville’s drawer bench is 20 in, and its 48 in bench is 24 in. Duramax’s bench is 24 in. There is no universal clearance. One Bold owner picked 18 in specifically so the car still parked.

How much weight can a garage cabinet workbench hold?

Use the number on the page for the piece you are buying, and read whether it says “evenly distributed.” Seville’s adjustable bench is 1,000 lb evenly distributed. The 11-drawer Seville bench is 500 lb total and 30 lb per drawer. Bold’s workbench is 500 lb, the locker 800 lb, and the wall cabinet 100 lb. The Bold FAQ then adds one locker, the wall cabinet, and the bench to 1,400 lb, which leaves out the second locker in the set. Armadillo’s set page prints no worktop load. The Gear Locker page says 750 lb, and the 3-drawer page says 500 lb total and 100 lb per drawer. Armadillo’s article says the standalone 56 in and 84 in workbenches “can support up to 1,500 lbs of evenly distributed weight.” That sentence is not the 7-piece set. Duramax publishes no load. A vise or an engine in the middle of a top is not an evenly distributed test.

Wood, stainless, or bamboo for the top?

On the Bold and Pro sets you choose bamboo or stainless. An r/garageporn owner with one of each uses stainless around cars and fluids because it scratches anyway, and bamboo when the work is wood. Two bamboo tops from the same brand can arrive different colors; that owner got a replacement. Seville’s tops in this check are polyurethane-coated wood or solid hardwood, not a bamboo-or-stainless choice. Duramax is acacia. Armadillo’s 7-piece top is hardwood, 1.5 in thick, and the set page does not name the species. Stainless will scratch and dent. That is a surface mark, not the same thing as sag. This check did not find owner reports of these particular tops sagging.

Can you put cabinets under a workbench?

Only if the cabinet is shorter than the clear space under the top, and only if the top is not already fastened to that cabinet. On The Garage Journal, a shop owner with an Edsal bench wrote that there was “32" to the underside of the bench top” and that a Harbor Freight end cabinet at 33 1/2 in was too tall to slide under it, even after thinking about removing the top lip. NewAge’s Pro FAQ says casters on a base cabinet keep that cabinet from fitting under an installed worktop. Bold’s FAQ says the workbench is designed to be attached to the locker and the wall, which is the opposite of a chest you roll underneath. Armadillo’s 3-drawer cabinet is 35.1 in high before any top goes on it, so it is a base the top sits on, not a box you slide under a separate bench.

Is 24-gauge steel enough?

The Bold title says 24-gauge. The Bold FAQ says 22 gauge frames and 24 gauge doors. Armadillo’s FAQ says 22 gauge frames, and the Gear Locker and 3-drawer pages say 22-gauge. Pro’s FAQ says 18 gauge frames and 19 gauge double-walled doors. Lower gauge numbers are thicker steel. Owners describe Bold as dentable and Pro as the stiffer line. “Enough” depends on whether you are storing garden tools or beating on the doors. Seville and Duramax did not publish a gauge on the pages checked.

Can I put the cabinets on wheels?

Seville’s 11-drawer bench includes locking 5 in wheels. NewAge says not to caster the lockers, and says a base cabinet on casters will not fit under an installed worktop. Bold’s workbench is supposed to be fastened to the locker and the wall, which is the opposite of a cart. Armadillo sells a wheel set as a separate piece. The 7-piece include list has brackets, not wheels.

Are these real woodworking benches?

No. They are steel cabinets with a top, or a rolling drawer bench at a fixed height. A woodworking bench is heavier, usually deeper than the tool wall you are reaching, and built so you can dog and vise a board. Use the height discussion. Do not use the product as a substitute for that bench.

Where do everyday tools go if the cabinets are for storage?

Drawers, if you bought a drawer bench. Hooks and a broom go on slatwall. Bins go on overhead racks. A ladder goes on a ladder hanger, not on top of a locker where the door track can catch it. Gauge and depth against Gladiator, without the worktop, are in the steel-cabinet comparison.
