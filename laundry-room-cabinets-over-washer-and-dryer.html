Cabinets Over a Washer and Dryer: Height, Depth, and the Best Wall Cabinets (2026)

The cabinet over a washer is a height problem first. A kitchen usually hangs uppers at 54 inches. A top-load lid, a pedestal, and a folding counter all break that number. Depth is the second decision: 12 inches over a counter you lean on, and something closer to 24 inches only when the machines are bare and you still need to reach the doors.

Short answer

Over front-loaders, put the bottom of the wall cabinets at 54–60 inches off the floor. That leaves about 13–21 inches above most full-size machines, which land around 38–41 inches tall. Over a top-loader, take the height with the lid open, add about 6 inches, and then use the maker’s overhead clearance if that number is higher. Captivating Cabinets walks a Samsung WA45N3050AW to about 65 inches. Do not build a fixed counter over that lid.

Pedestals change the picture. An LG pair on pedestals is about 54.7 inches tall in that same guide, so the uppers move to about 66 inches. A stacked pair starts a cabinet near 84 inches, which on an 8-foot ceiling is a shelf, not one of these 22- to 32-inch wall boxes. For a side-by-side pair, the pick is the Furnaza 60 in wall cabinet, about $249.99 (checked Nov 16, 2026): white shaker doors, and the listing describes two boxes, each 32 in high by 30 in wide by 12 in deep.

Clearance around the machines

TEC Cabinetry collects a practical set of gaps and is careful about what they are. About 1 in at each side. About 6 in behind, for hoses, the plug, and the vent. About 48 in in front of front-loaders, so a door can open and a person can stand there with a basket. About 24 in above a top-load washer so the lid can rise. TEC attributes the side, rear, and front figures to Whirlpool’s general sizing guidance and says the larger gaps are planning allowances, not universal rules and not code. The same article says not to put a fixed counter directly over a top-loader. Uppers can work if they clear the raised lid and still leave the water connections reachable.

Lowe’s measuring guide is the room, not the cabinet. It says a side-by-side pair needs a space at least 56 in wide, and a stacked unit needs about 78–80 in of vertical room. It also says to leave at least 1 in at the sides and about 6 in behind, and at least 20 in above a top-loader so the lid opens. That 20 in and TEC’s 24 in are both “the lid needs air.” Captivating’s method is stricter and more useful: measure the lid open, add about 6 in, and compare it with the number in the manual. Use the higher one. Lowe’s 78–80 in is the stack. It is not permission to start a wall cabinet at 80 in. The cabinet starts above whatever clearance that stack’s manual asks for, which is why the Samsung example lands near 84 in.

Two r/cabinetry threads are the owner version of the 18 in rule. People laying out a laundry counter with only 16 in up to the upper cabinets were worried a basket would not fit. Replies treated 18 in as the usual minimum and were clear there is no code number for it. Where someone actually stands and folds, the same kind of comment moves the opening toward 20–24 in. This page does not quote those threads. Eighteen inches is the planning minimum used here because it matches both that owner habit and Captivating’s counter sketch. It is still not a code clearance.

Width is the other tape measure. Lowe’s 56 in minimum for side-by-side machines, and Captivating’s note that a front-loading pair wants about 57–60 in of wall once the gaps are counted, are why a 60 in run is the useful cabinet and a 48 in pair is not. Add pedestals and a drain pan to the height before you order. Neither one is a surprise you can absorb after the cleat is on the wall.

12 in vs 24 in deep

Captivating’s depth rule is the one that decides which of these Amazon boxes is even the right shape. Over bare machines, the fronts are roughly 34–37 in out from the wall once hoses and the vent are behind them. A 12 in cabinet leaves you reaching across the appliance to grab the door. A 24 in deep wall cabinet, the kind kitchens use over a refrigerator, brings the doors forward. Over a folding counter you lean on, that deep box is in your face. Use 12 in there. The same guide says a folding counter itself often needs 30 in or more of depth, because a standard kitchen top stops short of the machine fronts. Support that top from the wall, a cleat into studs, or cabinets beside the machines. Do not rest it on the washer.

FAQ

How high above a washer and dryer should cabinets be?

Over front-loaders, 54–60 in from the floor to the bottom of the cabinet. That is Captivating’s planning range, and it leaves about 13–21 in over machines that are about 38–41 in tall. Over a top-loader, use the lid-open height plus about 6 in, or the overhead clearance in the manual, whichever is higher. Their Samsung WA45N3050AW example is about 65 in. These are not code minimums.

How high should cabinets be over a top-loading washer?

High enough that the open lid does not hit the box. Add about 6 in to the lid-open height for your hand and a bulky load, then check the manual’s overhead number and use the larger one. A flat 20 in (Lowe’s) or 24 in (TEC’s planning figure) above the closed machine is only a first guess. The Samsung example’s lid puts the cabinet near 65 in, which is more than 20 in above a 44 in machine.

What if the washer and dryer are on pedestals?

Add the pedestal before you mark the wall. Captivating’s LG pair is about 54.7 in on pedestals, and the uppers then start around 66 in. You will want a step stool for anything above the bottom shelf. Weekly supplies belong in a tall cabinet beside the machines. Include the pedestal in the order even if you buy it later. The cleat does not move.

How deep should cabinets over a washer and dryer be?

12 in over a counter you stand at and lean over. About 24 in over bare machines, so the doors come forward to your hands. Every wall cabinet in this guide’s table measured 12 in deep on the listing. The rattan “24 in” is the width of each box, not the depth. A 24 in deep upper is a cabinet-shop or kitchen-cabinet order, not one of these ASINs.

How much space do I need between a folding counter and the upper cabinets?

Plan on 18 in as the minimum people actually use, and 20–24 in if you stand there and fold. Owners on r/cabinetry who had drawn 16 in were already worried a basket would not fit, and the replies did not cite a code section. A counter at about 42 in plus an 18 in gap puts the cabinet bottom at 60 in, which is the top of the front-loader range, not the 54 in end.

Can I put a countertop over a top-load washer?

Not a fixed one. The lid has to open. TEC says to avoid that counter. Cabinets or a shallow shelf can sit higher, at the lid clearance, with the hoses still reachable. A folding counter is a front-loader project. Full-size front-loaders put that counter at about 41–43 in, often on a top 30 in or more deep, supported from the wall rather than from the machines.

Will a 60-inch cabinet fit over a side-by-side washer and dryer?

Lowe’s says the space should be at least 56 in wide. Captivating says a front-loading pair wants about 57–60 in of wall once the side gaps are counted. A 60 in run matches that. Two 24 in rattan cabinets are about 48 in and do not. Also leave about 1 in at the sides of the machines and about 6 in behind them for hoses and the vent, and do not assume a 27 in appliance is the whole opening.

Are these strong enough for detergent and towels?

Maybe. The Furnaza, FIMELLO, and rattan pages opened here did not publish a shelf rating this guide will repeat. The NEWOWNDS 30 in page uses 43 lb for the cabinet weight and for a maximum, which is not a rating. Bevivog claims 120 lb per shelf and about 300 lb for the pantry. Mount into studs.
