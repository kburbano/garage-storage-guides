Best Car Lift for a Low-Ceiling Garage (2026)

An 8, 9, or 10 ft garage can hold a car lift. It usually cannot hold a car stacked on another car. The useful buy is a portable low-rise or a short two-post. A 4-post is a storage lift, and the ceiling math starts over once a vehicle is on the runways.

Short answer

For working under a car in an 8–10 ft garage, the pick is the MaxJax MAX7 Standard if the slab can take anchors. BendPak’s MaxJax landing page says the lift can be used with ceilings as low as eight feet. This package is 7,000 lb, 110V at 23A, and was $4,880 on bendpak.com (list $5,395). The package page says the cylinder heads reach 95.75 in at full rise and the columns are 67.25 in. An 8 ft ceiling is 96 in, so “eight feet” is the column story, not a car at full height.

If you cannot drill the slab, buy the QuickJack 6000TL. It is 6,000 lb, about 24 in of lift with the adapters, and it was $1,640 (list $1,925). It does not stack cars. A 4-post such as the BendPak HD-9 ($5,610, list $6,095) has 88 in columns, so the steel can stand under 8 ft. Parking a second car needs the lifting height plus that vehicle’s height, plus room for the open door track. At 9 and 10 ft that sum usually fails. At 11–12 ft it becomes a measurement.

Who this is for

You want a car in the air in a home garage whose ceiling is about 8–12 ft, and the queries you typed look like “car lift for low ceiling garage,” “how much ceiling height is needed for a car lift,” or “4 post car lift for 9 foot ceiling.” The answer depends on the job. Service (wheels, brakes, oil) and storage (a second car parked on top) are different machines.

This page is not a ceiling rack and it is not a bin hoist. Bins and seasonal totes belong on overhead racks for low ceilings. A motor that lowers a platform of kayaks or bins is a motorized garage storage lift. Those hang from joists. A car lift stands on the slab.

What fits at 8, 9, 10, and 11–12 ft

Measure the finished ceiling at the lift, not the number on the builder’s brochure. Then open the door. The track and the opener hang into the number you just measured. One inch is 1/12 of a foot: 8 ft is 96 in, 9 ft is 108 in, 10 ft is 120 in, 11 ft is 132 in, 12 ft is 144 in.

8 ft (96 in)

  • QuickJack 6000TL. The rise is about two feet, so the ceiling is rarely the limit. A roof antenna, a roof rack, or a light fixture still can be. An r/Cartalk owner with about 8 ft 6 in of vertical clearance said a QuickJack was fine on a Focus and “a little hairy” on a truck and an Explorer because of adapters and antennas.
  • MaxJax MAX7. The columns are 67.25 in, so the posts are not the problem. At full rise the cylinder heads are 95.75 in, which leaves a quarter-inch under a perfect 96 in ceiling before the car’s roof, a light, or the door track. BendPak’s landing page still says ceilings as low as eight feet. Use a lower lock, and only on a vehicle you have measured. Seven lock positions are there for that reason.
  • Four-post columns can stand here. HD-9 columns are 88 in, Dannmar D4-9 columns are 89 in, Triumph NSS-8 overall height is 84 in. That is the steel, not a car on the runway. At full HD-9 rise the runway surface is 75 in up, which leaves 21 in for the vehicle. Almost no car is 21 in tall.

9 ft (108 in)

  • Service lifts still fit. Stacking still does not. An HD-9 at full rise leaves 108 − 75 = 33 in above the runway. A car does not fit in 33 in. You can lock the lift lower and work under a low vehicle. You do not get a second parking spot.
  • MaxJax cylinders have more air. 108 − 95.75 = 12.25 in above the cylinder heads at full rise. The roof of the car has to live in the bay too. This is a better service ceiling than 8 ft. It is not a full-height two-post ceiling.
  • Owners say the same thing. On r/AskMechanics (September 2025) one reply to a 9 ft, 110V garage was “9' ceilings aren't really enough for a 4 post.” Another said a 4-post still needs a jack on the runways to pull the wheels, and that second jack needs more height. On The Garage Journal, owners with 9 ft ceilings pointed at a mid-rise or scissor for oil, brakes, and tires, because a 4-post would not reach full height. One of them also said a BendPak HD-9 should fit that ceiling, which matches the 88 in columns and does not match two stacked cars.

10 ft (120 in)

  • Full HD-9 rise leaves 45 in above the runway (120 − 75). That is still shorter than most vehicles, so the top lock you can use is the one where the roof clears the track, not the top lock on the ladder.
  • Some people do park two low cars. A Garage Journal thread of 10 ft installs is the evidence, and it is picky. One owner said most door rails hang 18 in from the ceiling, which blocks double storage even with a convertible on top. Another, with two 1969 GTOs, could just fit them and could not stand straight under the car. Tracks on that door had been raised to about 8 in from the ceiling, with LiftMaster 3800 openers. A third shuts the door before lifting and marked the posts with tape so the stop point is obvious. One comment said to allow at least another 3–4 in so the locks can unlock.
  • An r/MechanicAdvice owner with a 10 ft ceiling put the 4-post in the single-door bay, removed the opener, and keeps the door closed whenever a vehicle is raised.

11–12 ft (132–144 in)

  • This is the first band where the HD-9’s full rise might clear a car. 132 − 75 = 57 in above the runway at 11 ft. 144 − 75 = 69 in at 12 ft. Those leftovers are before the open door. A low sports car can fit in the 11 ft leftover. A tall SUV often will not. Measure the vehicle you will actually park on the runways.
  • The Triumph’s published under-runway figure is 69 in at a 72 in lifting height. That is the room for the car parked on the floor, not the car on top. The car on top still needs its own height above the runway, plus clearance.
  • A full-rise two-post with a beam overhead is a different product from the MaxJax. This check did not price one. The low-ceiling queries do not land on that machine, and an 11–12 ft ceiling is where people start shopping it only after they have measured.

Ceiling clearance math

BendPak’s HD-9 installation manual (P/N 5900123) tells you the addition in one sentence: use the maximum lifting height of your model plus the height of the tallest vehicle you plan to raise. Then look for obstructions. That is the whole formula. The marketing line “it works with an 8-foot ceiling” is about whether the columns fit.

  • Four-post, car on the runways. Roof height ≈ lifting height at the lock you will use + the vehicle’s height. The ceiling, or the open door track, has to be higher than that roof by whatever gap you want. HD-9 max lifting height is 75 in. Dannmar D4-9 is also 75 in. Triumph NSS-8 is 72 in on top of the runway.
  • Four-post, car underneath. You need the underside of the runway higher than that car, with room to get the door open. Triumph publishes 69 in under the runway. The HD-9 product page publishes 70 in of max rise and 75 in of max lifting height, and it does not publish a separate under-runway number.
  • Two-post or scissor, car on pads. The car is already as tall as it is while it sits on its tires. The pads move the body up. On the MAX7 the pad goes from 5.25 in to 50.25 in with the pad high and one stackable adapter, which is 45 in of travel if you use all of it. Raised roof ≈ the car’s height + that travel, adjusted for where the jack point sits relative to the tire contact, + clearance. BendPak’s MAX7 page says to plan for the raised vehicle and the moving parts. It does not publish a “body rise” number. The 45 in figure is subtraction, not a spec. The older M7K install manual required 1 in of clearance above the tallest vehicle at maximum rise. That sentence is in the M7K manual, not on the MAX7 product page.
  • QuickJack. Adapter lift height is 24 in (26.75 in with the adapter and pin). The body moves up by roughly that amount from the collapsed frame, not by 24 in plus the car’s height. An 8 ft ceiling survives that. A low light fixture may not.
Work the leftovers before you believe a “fits 9 ft” line

HD-9 at full rise, runway at 75 in: 21 in left under an 8 ft ceiling, 33 in under 9 ft, 45 in under 10 ft, 57 in under 11 ft, 69 in under 12 ft. Those are inches above the runway, before the door. If your vehicle is taller than the leftover, you cannot use full rise. Lock lower, or buy a different lift.

Garage door, track, and opener

The ceiling you walk under with the door shut is not the ceiling you have with the door open. Standard trolley openers and horizontal tracks drop into the bay. Owners describe that drop as large.

  • About 18 in is a repeated owner number, not a code. On a Garage Journal 10 ft thread, one person wrote that most rails hang 18 in from the ceiling and that this blocks double storage even with a convertible on top. On an r/garageporn MaxJax post shot in an 8 ft garage, a commenter pointed out that the photo had no opener blocking about 18 in of height.
  • People move the door hardware instead of raising the roof. The same Garage Journal thread includes tracks reset to about 8 in from the ceiling and a wall-mount style opener (LiftMaster 3800 in that install). On r/MechanicAdvice, owners describe a jackshaft operator on the torsion shaft so the motor is not hanging over the car, and one owner simply removed the opener and closes the door before lifting.
  • Shutting the door is a real operating rule for some 10 ft installs, not a failure. It also means you cannot roll the door up while a car is at the lock that uses that space. Decide if you can live with that before the freight truck arrives.
  • Unlocking needs a little more travel. A Garage Journal comment on a BendPak install said to allow at least another 3–4 in of height so the locks can release. That is owner practice. Confirm it in the manual for the lift you buy. Do not design the bay so the roof touches the track at the top lock.

Concrete and 120V vs 220V

Portable does not mean unanchored. The MaxJax bolts down while a car is in the air, then the columns unbolt and roll away. The QuickJack frames are not a post lift. The product page checked tonight does not publish a slab thickness for them.

Slab

  • MaxJax. The concrete page on maxjax.com, written for the M7K, requires 4.25 in (108 mm), 3,000 PSI, fully cured, level, indoors, and free of cracks, deterioration, and expansion joints under the columns. The lift must be anchored whenever it is in use. The M7K install manual adds: do not drill a post-tensioned slab until qualified people have located the cables, keep anchors at least 6 in from seams and joints, and cure new concrete 28 days. The MAX7 still uses recessed drop-in anchors. Read the MAX7 manual before you treat the M7K page as the current spec. On r/garageporn, one commenter said a post-tensioned slab is the reason not to buy a MaxJax, and another guessed “6 in” of floor. The published minimum in this check is 4.25 in and 3,000 PSI. Guessing thicker is not the same as measuring.
  • BendPak HD-9. Manual 5900123: flat concrete only, not asphalt, no more than 3 degrees of slope, at least 4.25 in thick, 3,000 PSI, cured 28 days, and not within 6 in of cracked concrete or expansion joints. It says to check for a post-tension slab and contact the building architect before drilling. Anchors go one in each corner of each base plate.
  • Dannmar D4-9. The D4-9 series manual uses the same 4.25 in, 3,000 PSI, 28-day floor, and the same 3-degree slope limit. Do not install on cracked concrete.
  • BendPak MD-6XP. The installation manual calls it a portable mid-rise for narrower bays and low-ceiling garages, and still requires a dry, flat, stable floor: at least 4.25 in, 3,000 psi, cured 28 days if new, and not on or within 6 in of expansion seams or cracked concrete. The product page sells it as a lift you tow between bays. “Portable” here is not “any driveway.”
  • QuickJack 6000TL. Two frames, 78 lb each, 70 in long, 11 in wide, 3 in collapsed. No anchor spec was on the product page. Use a sound, flat floor and the manual. Do not read the silence as “a cracked 2 in slab is fine.”

Power

  • 110V can still be a new circuit. The MAX7 Standard package is 110V, 50–60 Hz, single-phase, 23A. That is above a common 15A or 20A receptacle. This page is not electrical advice. An Amazon reviewer on the Triumph NSS-8 listing reported a 110V cord that wanted 30A; a 28A generator would not even pull in the contactor, and a dedicated 30A circuit did. The Amazon description lists a 110V motor. Match the nameplate to a circuit, do not to a guess.
  • The HD-9 product page is 208–230V, 60 Hz, single-phase, 2 HP. If the garage has only 120V, this is the wrong default unless you are paying an electrician. The Dannmar D4-9 dealer specs list both: 110VAC, 20A, or 208–230VAC, 25A. Confirm which power unit is in the crate.
  • QuickJack volts were not in the 6000TL spec block checked tonight (it lists 2 HP and 70 dBA). BendPak’s portable-lift category page says most people use a 110V unit and that a 12V unit exists for a battery at the track. Confirm the unit in the cart.
  • The MD-6XP product page says 110V, 50/60 Hz, 1-phase. The manual says 115 or 220 VAC and “must specify at time of ordering,” with a 20A-or-greater fuse on 115V and a 25A-or-greater breaker on 230V. If those two documents disagree, the manual that ships with your serial number wins. Ask before you order.

Pros and cons

This is the category, not a single brand. A low ceiling makes the cheap mistake expensive: a 4-post that stands up and then cannot rise.

Pros

  • An 8 ft garage can still lift a car. QuickJack does it without anchors. MaxJax does it with the wheels off, if you accept a lower lock.
  • The short lifts give the floor back. Four-posts with caster kits can move, but they are still a parking space worth of steel.
  • 110V options exist: MaxJax Standard at 23A, QuickJack’s usual 110V unit, the MD-6XP product page, Dannmar’s 110V power unit, and the NSS-8 description.
  • Lock ladders exist so you can stop short of the ceiling. You are supposed to.

Cons

  • Stacking two cars at 9 or 10 ft is the claim that fails. Column height is the number in the ad. Vehicle height is the number that hits the track.
  • Anchored lifts want 4.25 in of 3,000 PSI concrete, cured, uncracked, and not post-tensioned unless a specialist clears the cables.
  • Freight is part of the price. The HD-9 ships at 1,616 lb. The NSS-8 listing says the driver does not unload it.
  • Door openers eat a foot or more. Fixing that is a door contractor, not a lift accessory.
  • “110V” on a 23A or 30A nameplate is a new circuit, not the outlet by the workbench.

Owner pain points

These are forum comments and listing reviews, not lab reports. The satisfied owners are in the same threads. A lift can be the right machine and still be a bad fit for the door you already have.

  • 8 ft and a QuickJack. r/Cartalk, March 2021: about 8 ft 6 in of clearance, fine on a Focus, “a little hairy” on a truck and an Explorer once adapters and antennas enter the picture. Another commenter said a 4-post will work and you will not get much use under it. A third preferred QuickJack to a low-ceiling two-post.
  • The two-foot complaint is the product. r/cars, October 2018: an owner of a 5,000 lb QuickJack (the older capacity) said it lifts about two feet and still recommended it, and pointed at MaxJax if you need more height. The current 6000TL’s adapter height is 24 in. The complaint did not go away when the capacity went up.
  • 9 ft, 110V, 4-post versus QuickJack. r/AskMechanics, September 2025: “9' ceilings aren't really enough for a 4 post.” The follow-up said pulling wheels on a 4-post means jacking the car again, which needs more ceiling, and that a scissor blocks work a post lift leaves open.   • MaxJax in an 8 ft garage, with the caveats in the comments. r/garageporn, March 2025: the photo post is enthusiastic. Who should skip it

  • You want two daily drivers stacked under 10 ft with the opener left where the builder put it. The leftovers above an HD-9 runway at full rise are 33 in at 9 ft and 45 in at 10 ft, and the track takes more. Buy a different building, or park one car somewhere else.
  • The slab is unknown, cracked, or post-tensioned and you are not going to measure it or hire someone who can locate cables. Skip MaxJax, the HD-9, and the Dannmar. A QuickJack does not solve a slab you should not stand a heavy frame on, but it does not ask for anchors.
  • You rent, and the lease forbids anchors. The MaxJax’s portability starts after the holes are drilled. Those holes stay.
  • The only job is an oil change on a car you can reach with ramps. The QuickJack is the upgrade when ramps are not enough. A $5,610 four-post is not the upgrade.
  • You need to stand up under the car. None of the low-ceiling service lifts in this check create a standing-height bay under an 8 or 9 ft ceiling. That is a taller building.
  • The storage problem is bins, bikes, or a ladder, not a vehicle. Use overhead racks, a motorized storage lift, bike storage that does not drill the slab, or ladder storage. Tools you grab while the car is down belong on slatwall.

FAQ

How much ceiling height is needed for a car lift?

For a four-post, BendPak’s HD-9 manual says to add the model’s maximum lifting height to the height of the tallest vehicle, then check overhead obstructions. The HD-9’s max lifting height is 75 in and its columns are 88 in. The columns can fit an 8 ft (96 in) ceiling. A car on the runways at full rise cannot. For the MaxJax MAX7, the cylinder heads reach 95.75 in at full rise, so an 8 ft ceiling is essentially touching the lift before the roof of the car is counted. For a QuickJack 6000TL, the listed adapter height is 24 in, and the ceiling is rarely the limit.

How high does a garage ceiling need to be for a 4-post car lift?

High enough for the columns, and separately high enough for lifting height plus the vehicle plus the open door. In this check the column heights are 84 in (Triumph NSS-8), 88 in (BendPak HD-9), and 89 in (Dannmar D4-9). Full-rise leftovers above a 75 in runway are 33 in at 9 ft, 45 in at 10 ft, 57 in at 11 ft, and 69 in at 12 ft, before the track. Two normal cars start to be a measurement at 11–12 ft, not at 9.

Will a 4-post car lift fit a 9 ft ceiling?

The steel will. An HD-9 column is 88 in and a 9 ft ceiling is 108 in. You will not get full rise with a car on the runways: 108 − 75 leaves 33 in for that vehicle. Owners on r/AskMechanics and Garage Journal say a 9 ft ceiling is the wrong place to expect a 4-post to work at full height. A QuickJack or a MaxJax is the service tool in that bay.

Will a 4-post car lift fit a 10 ft ceiling?

Often, as a partial-rise install, and sometimes as stacked parking for low cars after the door is rebuilt. Full HD-9 rise leaves 45 in above the runway (120 − 75). Garage Journal owners with 10 ft ceilings report success with low cars, tracks moved closer to the ceiling, a wall-mount opener, or a rule that the door stays shut while the lift is up. One of them could not stand straight under two GTOs. Copy the measurement, not the photo.

What is the best portable car lift for a small garage?

The QuickJack 6000TL if you want no anchors and can work at about 24 in: $1,640 on bendpak.com in this check, 6,000 lb, frames that store off to the side. The MaxJax MAX7 Standard if you want a two-post and the slab qualifies: $4,880, 7,000 lb, columns that unbolt, with the 95.75 in full-rise height treated as a limit. The MD-6XP ($3,310, 47 in) sits between them and puts a scissor under the car.

Do I need 120V or 220V?

The MAX7 Standard package is 110V at 23A. The HD-9 product page is 208–230V. The Dannmar D4-9 dealer spec offers 110V at 20A or 208–230V at 25A. The NSS-8 Amazon description says 110V, and a reviewer needed a 30A circuit. The 6000TL spec block did not list volts; BendPak’s portable-lift page describes a 110V unit and an optional 12V unit. “Household power” is not a specification. Read the nameplate.

How thick does the concrete need to be?

For the anchored lifts in this check, the published minimum is 4.25 in and 3,000 PSI, cured (28 days in the HD-9, Dannmar, MD-6XP, and M7K manuals), flat, and not cracked. Keep anchors off expansion joints. Do not drill a post-tensioned slab until the cables are located. The QuickJack 6000TL page does not publish a thickness. The MaxJax concrete page is written for the M7K; confirm the MAX7 manual before you drill.
